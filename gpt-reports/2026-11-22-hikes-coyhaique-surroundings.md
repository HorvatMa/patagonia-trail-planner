## A. Candidates (best first)

**1. Monumento Natural Dos Lagunas: Laguna Escondida and Laguna El Toro circuit. Verdict MAYBE (closest to ADD).**
- Access: east of Coyhaique on the road towards Paso Coyhaique/Alto, roughly 25–30 km [estimate]. Mostly paved, then gravel. 2WD is fine [estimate]. OSM has the monument at -45.529, -71.839.
- Walk: about 6 km, 150–250 m gain, 2–3 h [estimate; a tour listing says ≈6 km, "medium-low", Expedia, undated]. Lonely Planet describes a short trail to Laguna El Toro and a longer loop along the north shore of Laguna Escondida, plus a 1 km self-guided trail near the entrance.
- SAC T1–T2, no hazards. Shorter than the 3 h preference, but a good quiet half day.
- Crowds in Jan–Feb: low to moderate. It is a roadside wetland with day visitors, and a small CONAF post may be staffed. [estimate]
- Fees/camping: a small CONAF fee is possible. No official camping is known. Pull-offs on the gravel roads are the car option, but check the monument's rules, because it is a protected area. [estimate]
- Confidence: low-medium.

**2. Cerro Mackay summit. Verdict MAYBE (navigation and a rock-face risk; cut it if the ravine route looks like scrambling).**
- Access: the peak sits just south of Coyhaique (OSM -45.608, -72.075). The start is a forest road on the south side of town. I have no verified trailhead, so the coordinates below are only the peak. Paved to town, then a short gravel or forest road [estimate].
- Walk: Komoot lists the summit at 1,220 m, which means about 800–900 m gain from the town side, so roughly 10–14 km and 5–7 h return [estimate]. Komoot comments say the stored path is not accessible. Users advise keeping left of the ravine after the forest road; the Komoot line ends at a rock face, while the left path is marked with coloured ribbons. [web, Komoot undated]
- SAC T3 (conservative). Hazards: navigation, steep-loose. I give no scramble tag, but the rock-face comment is a red flag. The summit crags are a popular climbing area (sport routes), so do not follow climbing approaches.
- Crowds: low. Locals and climbers only. Views over Coyhaique and the Simpson valley.
- Camping: none on the hill. The car can sleep at Coyhaique campgrounds or at the edge of the reserve.
- Confidence: low.

**3. Lago Frío / Lago Pollux area, south-east of Cerro Mackay. Verdict MAYBE (lakeside walks, not a real hike; low confidence).**
- Access: a gravel road from the Mackay area to Frío and Pollux, 30–50 km from Coyhaique [estimate]. Travel guides describe pampa-type lakes with sparse vegetation. OSM: Lago Pollux -45.663, -71.837 or -45.675, -71.859 (two matches); Lago Frío -45.561, -71.864 or -45.664, -71.946 (two matches, unclear which is the right one). I found no marked trail, so it would be easy off-trail walking around the lakes and nearby low hills in open steppe.
- Walk: 8–15 km, 100–400 m gain, 3–5 h [estimate]. SAC T2, navigation. Fences and private land are likely on steppe roads, so check access gates. Flag private-land.
- Crowds: very low.
- Camping: wild car camping on the gravel roads is probably possible if the land is public. Verify.
- Confidence: low.

**4. Laguna Verde one-night tent trip (RN Coyhaique CONAF campground). Verdict MAYBE (overlaps catalogue hike `coy`).**
- Access: the same trailhead as `coy`. About 5 km from town by gravel; OSM puts Laguna Verde at -45.5365, -72.0165, which matches the catalogue point. Walk in about 3.9 km each way [web, Andeshandbook, undated]. A reserve guide lists a rustic 6-site campground at Laguna Verde (roughly US$7 for up to six people, from an older guide) and a 10-site one at Casa Bruja [web, older guide, price unverified].
- Total: about 8 km, 150–300 m gain over 2 days [estimate]. SAC T1–T2.
- Extension: the Cerro Cinchao loop is about 17.9 km with roughly 1,000 m gain per a Wikiloc user log [web, undated]. It sits beside `coy` and is not a separate hike. Booking: the catalogue notes that RN Coyhaique is closed on Mondays and booked via pasesparques.cl. Check whether camping needs a separate booking.
- Crowds: moderate by day, quiet by evening. It is near town and visited by locals on weekends.
- Confidence: medium on the walk, low on current camping rules.

**5. RN Río Simpson: Cerro Huemules / Río Claro sector trail from the ranger station. Verdict MAYBE (low confidence).**
- Access: one source says a trail starting at the ranger station takes about 3 h, and that the Río Claro sector is 16 km from Coyhaique on gravel and needs a 4x4 [web, undated]. Another source places the Área de Protección Cerro Huemules inside RN Río Simpson. I could not confirm the trailhead, the distance or whether it is the same trail. This sector is not the famous Cascada La Virgen (catalogue `simpson`).
- Estimate: 6–10 km, 300–500 m gain, about 3 h. SAC T2. A huemul sighting is mentioned in the sources; do not approach.
- Crowds: low. Confidence: low.

## B. Notes
- Region-wide: the gravel roads east of Coyhaique are steppe with fences, so private land is likely. Wind is strong, and weather changes quickly. Nothing here is above T3 as far as I can tell.
- Fuel: fill up in Coyhaique. Balmaceda airport is about 55 km south-east of town.
- Sleeping: Coyhaique has campgrounds and cabins. RN Coyhaique has CONAF campgrounds (see candidate 4). I did not verify wild-camping rules; ask CONAF about the reserves and the monument.
- The catalogue only covers the `coy` loop and the `simpson` falls, so the other places here are still open.
- Best next step: ask CONAF Aysén or the Coyhaique tourist office for a track of candidates 1, 2 and 5, and download Wikiloc tracks before approving.
