# GPT36H–37H: Cochrane – Ruta de los Pioneros – Villa O'Higgins

*Scouting report by a research subagent, 22 Nov 2026. Source: GPT Hikers Manual (Oct 2025) plus web sources listed below. Verdicts and figures are the agent's; verify before relying on them.*

## GPT36H / GPT37H scouting (Cochrane – Villa O'Higgins)

Summary: there are no strong ADDs. GPT36H is a remote multi-day horse trail whose ends are 50 km of gravel road, and the manual has no per-km text for it. Pages 787–790 are only an overview map and elevation profiles. GPT37H is well documented, but it is a 4–9 day circuit. Only one stretch works as a day hike from a car: the Portezuelo Los Corrales climb from Puerto Bahmondes. The Calluqueo area near Cochrane is the second-best option.

### Portezuelo Los Corrales from Puerto Bahmondes (Península La Florida)  — verdict: MAYBE (leaning ADD)
- GPT ref: GPT37H regular route km 7.6 → 15.1 (Puerto Bahmondes → hydro station → ascent along Río Salto → pass at 1,159 m). Manual pp. 808–809 and 817–819; elevation profile p. 797.
- Car access point: Puerto Bahmondes, the end of the Carretera Austral, 7.6 km of good wide gravel south of Villa O'Higgins (about -48.53, -72.58, estimated from the map; check on a map app). No 4x4 needed. Tourists drive here every afternoon.
- Hike: out-and-back, about 15 km and about 950–1,000 m ascent (270 m → 1,159 m), about 6–7 h.
- Terrain:
  - Trail of variable visibility, often split into several branches. Around km 10.5–12.4 it becomes vague animal tracks in forest, with several crossings of Río Salto. The manual calls these "easily fordable"; Sean & Neele (Feb 2025) found them knee-deep after a rainy day.
  - The turn-off at km 12.4 is "only vaguely visible", so a GPS track is mandatory.
  - The final climb is steep switchbacks on loose gravel. The pass itself is broad and open, with no exposure.
  - Snow patches at the pass in December 2023 and about 5 cm of fresh snow in Feb 2025. Usually fine in February.
  - Navigation: moderate.
- Fees / private land / permits: none. The route goes through the Villa O'Higgins hydro station, which has a "personal no autorizado" sign and a camera. The manual says hikers crossing the one bridge without lingering aren't bothered. Hikers in Feb 2024 and Nov 2024 passed with no problems. Technically this means walking past a no-entry sign.
- Current status: wikiexplora reports from 2024–2026 show the route in use. Sean & Neele (Feb 2025) said this pass has "better views and a better trail" than the eastern side of the circuit.
- Why it's worth it: it is the only real alpine-meadow day hike near Villa O'Higgins. From the pass you look north over Lago O'Higgins and the village, and south into a valley lined with glaciers (Cerro Taitao). It is longer and higher than the Cerro Santiago/Altavista walks already in the plan.
- Fits: Plan 1, Feb 10 (replace the Villa O'Higgins flex day). Extra driving about 15 km.
- Confidence: medium. Uncertain: the exact vagueness of the trail in the forest section, and whether staff at the hydro station ever turn people back.

### Glaciar Calluqueo: Option 22A glacier trail + Mirador Laguna Calluqueo  — verdict: MAYBE
- GPT ref: GPT36H regular route northern trailhead at km 50.0 (Laguna Calluqueo, X {36H}[50.0/553]), plus Option 22A (called OH36H-24A in older track files) to the glacier front. The manual mentions this only in the commentary on p. 418; there are no detail pages.
- Car access point: road X-901. Leave the Carretera Austral about 5 km south of Cochrane, signed "San Lorenzo". Then about 45 km of gravel to Mirador Calluqueo (-47.597, -72.497). A Feb 2024 report says the road is "very good, fine for non-4x4". The road ends at a gate on private land just past the mirador. A Mar 2025 report says hikers don't need to pass that gate. About 1–1.5 h each way from Cochrane.
- Hike: out-and-back, roughly 12–14 km. Joscha (Mar 2024) put the glacier view at about km 3 and the end at about km 6. Ascent is estimated at 300–500 m; no reliable figure was found. About 4–6 h return.
- Terrain:
  - Mostly forest trail. About 140 m from the start there is an unmarked ford of Río Pedregoso: knee-deep, milky glacial water, "fair current" (Michael & Kasia, Mar 2025).
  - The same report describes a "narrow exposed traverse of ~400 m" on a forested ridge, then a lookout about 150 m past the end of the trail.
  - Both of these are at or near the couple's hard limits.
  - The drive-up Mirador Laguna Calluqueo alone (views of the lake, the glacier and Monte San Lorenzo) has no such issues.
- Fees / private land / permits: none reported for the trail. Tours from Cochrane reach the glacier by boat across the lagoon, but a car and your own feet are enough. Glaciares Chilenos also says the viewpoint can be reached "by own means".
- Current status: used by many GPT hikers in 2024–2026. Kole (Jan 2026) saw several tourist cars at the trailhead in clear weather. iso-maps lists a separate "Sendero Glaciar Calluqueo" of 6 km and +640 m up to 1,103 m (a moraine or ridge viewpoint). Its start point and route are unclear, and a Wikiloc track describing a moraine walk to a viewpoint could not be opened.
- Why it's worth it: a large glacier under Monte San Lorenzo without a tour, at no cost, and a different experience from Tamango.
- Fits: Plan 1, Feb 13 (swap with Tamango or do both on one long day, since the mirador is a 1.5 h drive) or Feb 12 afternoon. Plan 2, Feb 9 (replace the buffer). Extra driving about 100 km round trip on gravel.
- Confidence: medium-low on the trail itself (the ford and the exposed traverse need checking locally; a guide office in Cochrane would know). High on the drive-up viewpoint.

### Refugio Toni Röhrer / Campo De Agostini (Río Tranquilo upper valley, Monte San Lorenzo, Chilean side)  — verdict: MAYBE (as a 1-night trip only)
- GPT ref: not in the manual text. Wikiexplora's GPT36H page lists it as a 2025 exploration around GPT36H (upper Río Tranquilo valley). This is the Chilean approach to Monte San Lorenzo.
- Car access point: Fundo San Lorenzo, home of the Soto family, via roads X-901 and X-903. About 2.5 h from Cochrane (wikiexplora). Older blogs say the last stretch is a rutted track that needs 4x4. One blog (likely 2016) mentions a river ford, "not to the waist". It is unclear whether cars have to drive through it, which is a rental-insurance problem.
- Hike: about 9.5 km and +325 m each way to the hut (2016 report). About 2 h (Thijmen & Volker, Mar 2025) to 3–4 h (older blogs). As a day trip that is about 19 km and 650 m, but with 5 h of driving it only makes sense as a 1-night trip: hut or camp, then a day hike toward the glacier or lake, then out.
- Terrain: trail with a stream crossing in a canyon. Beyond the hut, Paso del Comedor (about 2,000 m, snow, difficult navigation) is out of bounds.
- Fees / private land / permits: private land. The hut is 7,000 CLP per person (Mar 2025). An undated iOverlander note says camping is about 10,000 CLP per person. There may be a day-use fee; ask the family.
- Current status: the Soto family was operating the hut in March 2025. No 2024–26 information on the road.
- Why it's worth it: a close view of Patagonia's second-highest peak, with almost no visitors.
- Fits: Plan 2, Feb 8–9 (replace Tamango + buffer, with Tamango moved elsewhere). Extra driving about 300 km round trip on poor gravel.
- Confidence: low (road condition and any ford, fees, whether day visitors are welcome).

### Lago Christie trailhead, southern end of Ruta de los Pioneros  — verdict: MAYBE (low priority)
- GPT ref: GPT36H regular route around km 142 (Lago Christie road end) heading north. Commentary on pp. 418–419; profile p. 789.
- Car access point: end of the gravel road at the south end of Lago Christie, roughly 45–50 km from Villa O'Higgins along the Río Mayer valley. About 1.5–2 h each way. Coordinates not found. A subsidised bus runs about 4–10 times a month (600–3,500 CLP).
- Hike: out-and-back, flexible length, e.g. 8–10 km each way. Ascent is modest (the profile shows 450–680 m altitude with small bumps).
- Terrain: "big sign and a picnic table at the start, trail clearly defined" (Mar 2024); "mellow, in good shape, next to no mud" (Feb/Mar 2024). Further north there are fallen trees and overgrowth (2025–26 reports).
- Fees / private land / permits: none reported. Settlers live along the route; be courteous. Reports mention dogs at some puestos.
- Current status: walked by many GPT hikers 2024–2026.
- Why it's worth it: a quiet historic pioneer trail beside a remote lake. Scenically weaker than Los Corrales, and 3–4 h of driving for an ordinary forest/lake walk.
- Fits: Plan 1, Feb 10 as an alternative to Los Corrales (bad-weather fallback, since it stays low).
- Confidence: medium on the trail, low on the road (no source gives road condition or distance; ask locally).

### Laguna Verde 1-night (GPT37H "North Circuit" or out-and-back via Portezuelo El Rose)  — verdict: NO
- GPT ref: GPT37H km 7.6–30.3 and 104.7–124.9. Manual pp. 805, 814, 819–821 and 833–835.
- Reasons:
  - The loop is about 38–52 km with about 2,000–2,400 m of ascent, and has fords beyond the couple's limit. Ford at km 21.6: "as deep as my whole leg" (Dec 2024), knee-deep and strong (Feb 2025). Río San Martín ford: knee to mid-thigh (2025–26).
  - Going out and back via El Rose avoids those fords, but means about 17 km and over 1,000 m each way, then a steep loose 700 m descent and "difficult navigation, swampy" ground after the pass (Mar 2025).
  - 2026 reports: "one big swamp", overgrown.

### Península La Florida full or half circuit by boat (Bahía Pescado)  — verdict: NO
- GPT ref: GPT37H variations "West/East Traverse" (3–5 days) and "South Circuit". Manual pp. 805 and 811–816.
- Reasons:
  - No regular boat service. You ask to be dropped off from the glacier tour, which costs 55–70k CLP per person one way (Ruedas de la Patagonia / Amigo Patagón, 2025).
  - The tour is cancelled often because of wind. The manual warns of waiting "a few days or even a week".
  - Every variation is multi-day with demanding fords and navigation. The settlers at Bahía Pescado were absent in Jan/Feb 2026.
  - Not realistic for unguided budget day hikers.

### Monte San Lorenzo Argentine-side options (GPT36H Options 14–20)  — verdict: NO
These are long cross-country exploration routes across the border (manual p. 420). They need a border safe-conduct, and Plan 3's Paso Roballos day leaves no time for them.

### Other things the couple should know
- **Cash:** Villa O'Higgins has no ATM (manual p. 806). Bring cash from Cochrane or Coyhaique; most shops do take cards.
- **Glaciar Mosco trail (Villa O'Higgins):** damaged by floods. "Not yet reopened" in Dec 2023, and "closed… only with a guide" in Mar 2025 (wikiexplora). Don't count on it for the flex day.
- **Altavista / Mirador Glaciares Mosco y Huemul:** a free trail with no fee, crossing private land. It starts just after the Mayer bridge, about 3.5 km from the village, and goes up to about 11 km return / 400 m. It is a good easy complement if the "Cerro Santiago trails" in Plan 1 turn out to be short.
- **Lago O'Higgins and wind:** weather on the lake is the main uncertainty. It does not affect the car plan.
- **Laguna San Rafael confusion:** PN Patagonia / Tamango is outside GPT36H (it is GPT35), so it was not assessed here.
- **Plans:** I found nothing in the manual contradicting the Cochrane, Tortel or Villa O'Higgins plans. Calluqueo is the obvious free alternative to the paid Calluqueo tours sold in Cochrane.
