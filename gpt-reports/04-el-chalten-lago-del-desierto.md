# GPT38–40: El Chaltén and Lago del Desierto

*Scouting report by a research subagent, 22 Nov 2026. Source: GPT Hikers Manual (Oct 2025) plus web sources listed below. Verdicts and figures are the agent's; verify before relying on them.*

### Lago del Desierto eastern shore + Laguna Larga / Hito (GPT39 start + last bit of GPT38 Option 1)  — verdict: ADD
- GPT ref: GPT39 regular route km 0 to about 12–14 (Gendarmería at Punta Norte → Punta Sur, the trail along the eastern shore). The Laguna Larga / Hito stretch is the last part of the GPT38 approach on the Argentine side (Option 1 "Carretera", the road from Candelario Mancilla). Manual pp. 422–423 (commentary), 552–553, 846–849 (map and elevation profile). The detail pages say "To be updated" and contain almost no route text.
- Car access point: Punta Sur at the south end of Lago del Desierto, about 37 km from El Chaltén on RP41, approx −49.03, −72.87. Gravel road, no 4x4 needed, free parking.
- Hike, three versions:
  (a) Walk the shore trail out and back: about 24–28 km, about 700–900 m ascent (the profile undulates between 510 and 660 m), 8–9 h. This sits at the upper edge of the limits.
  (b) Better: take the boat out (leaves Punta Sur about 10:00, about 50 min), walk to Laguna Larga and the border marker and back (about 10–12 km, about 220 m), then either catch the 17:30 boat back or walk back along the shore (another 12–14 km). That gives about 22–26 km and about 600–800 m.
  (c) Reverse of (b): leave early on foot along the shore, do Laguna Larga, and take the 17:30 boat back.
  One car is enough, because the boat replaces a shuttle.
- Terrain: Marked trail through forest. The shore trail is narrow and constantly up and down, and one recent hiker took 5.5 h for about 15 km. No fords and no exposure. Navigation is easy.
- Fees / private land / permits: The lake is in a provincial reserve, not the national park, so no APN fee. Walking is free. The boat is paid, and I found no reliable current fare: listings show ARS 16,000 and 45,000, both undated and probably stale. Buy through an agency in El Chaltén. They must carry passports and register at the Gendarmería at Punta Norte, and must not continue past the border marker.
- Current status (web): Boat runs in high season, 10:00 out and 17:30 back (elchalten.com, undated). One booking page says it does not run on Wednesdays. Several crossing blogs from about 2023–25 confirm the shore trail is used by hikers coming from Villa O'Higgins. Laguna Larga / Hito is listed as about 10 km, 4–5 h, moderate, free (elchalten.com).
- Why it's worth it: Fitz Roy seen from the north across the lake, Laguna Larga and the Vespignani glacier area, far fewer people than the El Chaltén trails, and no park fee. It easily shares the drive with Glaciar Huemul and Chorrillo del Salto, which are already in the plans.
- Fits: Plan 3, Feb 11 (flex day): replace the "Lago del Desierto / Glaciar Huemul" item, and keep Huemul (short, about 2–4 km) only if time allows. Plan 4, Feb 11 catch-up day: same swap. No extra driving (the 75 km round trip is already planned).
- Confidence: medium. Uncertain are the boat price, the days it runs and whether one-way walk-on tickets are sold (ask in town), and the exact shore distance (sources say 12–15 km).

### Río Eléctrico → Piedra del Fraile → Lago Eléctrico (GPT39 option, seen on the map only)  — verdict: MAYBE
- GPT ref: A thin red option line runs west from GPT39 at the Río Eléctrico / El Pilar junction up to Lago Eléctrico (Satellite Image 77, p. 847). The text names no options, so I can't confirm its option number.
- Car access point: Río Eléctrico bridge on RP41, about 17 km north of El Chaltén, approx −49.227, −72.940. Gravel road, no 4x4. APN lists "Río Eléctrico" as one of its paid entry points in the north zone.
- Hike: out and back, about 20 km to the lake head, about 250–350 m ascent, about 6–7 h. Piedra del Fraile alone is about 13 km round trip and about 100 m.
- Terrain: Forest and valley trail, easy, no exposure. Glaciar Pollone is a further 4–5 h round trip over moraine, which is beyond what they should do.
- Fees / private land / permits: Piedra del Fraile is a private reserve with a refugio; elchalten.com says the trail is "free / private property". The APN entry fee probably applies at the Río Eléctrico portal.
- Current status (web): No 2025–26 reports found; Hiking Project data are from 2016.
- Why it's worth it: A quiet glacier-valley day under the north side of Fitz Roy, at the edge of the icefield, with much smaller crowds than Laguna de los Tres.
- Fits: Plan 3, as the replacement for Laguna Toro (see the next entry) or on Feb 11 if Lago del Desierto is not chosen. Extra driving about 34 km.
- Confidence: low–medium. I couldn't confirm the route from the manual text, and current fees and conditions are unverified.

### Laguna Toro (GPT40 km 0–16, start of the Huemul Circuit)  — verdict: NO as a day hike (MAYBE as one night)
- GPT ref: GPT40 regular route km 0 to about 16, profile p. 852 (400 m → about 1,050 m at km 8 → about 600 m at Laguna Toro).
- Car access point: El Chaltén, at the visitor centre / trailhead at the south end of town.
- Hike: About 32 km out and back and about 1,100 m ascent, so it breaks the 25 km limit. The official APN leaflet gives 16 km one way, 7 h, compulsory registration and "sendero sin marcar" (unmarked trail), and a hiker report says the cairns are easy to lose. As one night it works: camping at Laguna Toro is free with registration.
- Alternative: out and back to the high point at about km 8 (about 16 km, about 700 m), which looks down into the Río Túnel valley. Its views largely overlap Pliegue Tumbado.
- Current status: On 4 Jan 2026 a fire of about 340 ha broke out on Cerro Huemul, starting near the lower Río Túnel Tyrolean, close to Laguna Toro. The Huemul Circuit was closed and the Laguna Toro campers were sent back (La Nación, 5 Jan 2026). Check the current state of the trail at the APN office.
- Fits: Plan 3, Feb 10. Replace it with Lago Eléctrico or Lago del Desierto, or make it a one-night trip.
- Confidence: high on the distance problem.

### Glaciar Viedma views from Bahía Túnel / Paso Huemul (GPT40 km about 38–57)  — verdict: NO
Paso Huemul (about 1,000 m, the glacier viewpoint) is about 17–18 km one way from Bahía Túnel. An operator gives 18 km and 6–7 h for that stretch, so the out-and-back is about 36 km, with private estancia land at the bottom. The only legal way to see the glacier as a day trip is a paid boat (Bahía Túnel → Bahía Cabo de Hornos, or the guided "Viedma Ice Trek"), which is outside the brief. The full circuit also needs the Tyrolean traverses, so it is excluded.

### GPT38 Argentine side (Paso del Diablo, Glaciar Chico)  — verdict: NO
Everything beyond Laguna Larga is border no-man's-land. The manual itself says the scenic route over Paso del Diablo (834 m) is not an officially permitted border route (pp. 156–157, 422). It is also more than 25 km from Punta Norte, which can only be reached by boat or the 12–14 km shore trail.

**Other things the couple should know**
- **Trip year:** The brief says Jan–Feb 2026, but today is 22 Nov 2026, so I assume they mean 2027 and fees may rise again.
- **Park fees (APN official table, valid from 1 Jun 2026, same for the north zone and the El Chaltén entrance):**
  - Daily pass ARS 50,000 per person; the second day is 50% off within 72 h.
  - Flexipass ARS 100,000 for 3 days or ARS 175,500 for 7 days. For 5–6 hiking days in Plan 3, the 7-day pass is the cheapest.
  - Payment is online or by QR code at the entry points (ventaweb.apn.gob.ar), card only, no cash.
  - Lago del Desierto and Glaciar Huemul are outside the park. The Huemul trail charges its own private fee, about US$9 in 2026; some sources say cash only.
  - Since 3 Mar 2026 El Chaltén also charges a municipal tourist tax of about ARS 3,000 per person on visitors who don't stay overnight, including those arriving by car. How it applies to people sleeping in a car is unclear.
- **Sleeping in the car:** I found no official rule. iOverlander reports say vans and campers park at the visitor centre and the Fitz Roy trailhead without trouble; the "no camping" signs are aimed at tents (user reports, undated). Paid campsites exist in town.
- **Laguna Torre:** CONICET scientists have flagged a slope on Cerro Solo sliding into Lago Torre, with a possible outburst flood (Aug–Sep 2026 press). There is no trail closure yet; check the APN visitor centre for status and possible reroutes.
- **Earlier closures:** In 2024 the last stretch to Laguna de los Tres and the whole Pliegue Tumbado trail were temporarily closed because of staff cuts. Nothing current was found.
- **Plan errors:** Laguna Toro is not a day hike within their limits (32 km), and the flex day's "Lago del Desierto" item is best done as the boat-plus-shore-trail day above.
